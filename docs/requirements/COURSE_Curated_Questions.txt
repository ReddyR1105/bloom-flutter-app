I · Widgets & implementation
I1 · Widget tree & ownership Required
Show one reusable StatelessWidget and one StatefulWidget in your app. Explain their responsibilities, how build() renders current values, and which interaction requires mutable state.

Evidence: widget tree, actual classes, and a before/after UI action.

I2 · Buttons, controls & state Required
Trace a button and slider interaction (or justify your numeric-control alternative) through input validation, setState()/state notification, and rendering. Which values are transient and which must be saved?

Evidence: callbacks, form validation, visible values, and saved settings/records.

I3 · Five-screen user flow
Demonstrate catalog → editor → detail/activity → history → settings. How do routing, back navigation, lists/grids, and a gesture preserve the user's context?

Evidence: screen map, route code, and a complete user flow.

I4 · Build order & redesign
Explain the implementation order of your two record types and activity flow. Which peer observation changed a widget/layout, and how did you verify that revision?

Evidence: commits, before/after wireframes, and peer task results.

I5 · Accessibility under change
Show portrait/landscape, increased text scale, and reduced-motion behavior. How do labels, focus, dialogs, and non-color status feedback keep your activity usable?

Evidence: device settings, labeled controls, and accessibility test notes.

A · Architecture & state
A1 · Responsibilities
Trace one feature through widget, state/model, and data helper. Where are domain rules and database calls owned, and what would break if the widget performed every responsibility?

Evidence: architecture diagram and file-level walkthrough.

A2 · Lifecycle
Show how a controller, timer, listener, or async operation is owned and cleaned up. What happens when a user leaves the screen before an operation finishes?

Evidence: initState/dispose or equivalent ownership code and a navigation-away test.

A3 · State-management trade-off
Compare your chosen state approach with one alternative. Demonstrate loading/error/success and cross-screen updates. Graduate students must show their Provider, Riverpod, or BLoC implementation, not setState alone.

Evidence: state transitions, rationale, and a reproducible consistency test.

A4 · Testable boundaries
How do repository boundaries and dependency injection let you test without a real device database? Show the dependency direction and a fake or test implementation.

Evidence: ARCHITECTURE.md, injected dependency, and test output.

A5 · Refactor with constraints
Identify a fragile responsibility and propose ordered refactor steps. How would you preserve CRUD, offline behavior, navigation, and the current acceptance criteria?

Evidence: current code, concrete revision plan, and regression checks.

D · Local storage & CRUD · no cloud backend
D1 · Both entities' CRUD Required
Show create, read, update, and delete for your primary record and linked history record. Trace SQL/helper calls, validation, delete confirmation, and the final refreshed UI. Explain what happens to child records when a parent is deleted.

Evidence: schema/keys, parameterized operations, and both complete CRUD demonstrations.

D2 · Persistence & no-cloud proof Required
Explain SQLite versus SharedPreferences in your app. With networking disabled, save records and a setting, close/relaunch the app, and show restored values. Why are GitHub, ClickUp, and YouTube not app backends?

Evidence: dependencies, local file/schema ownership, airplane-mode restart, and restored setting.

D3 · Data integrity
Demonstrate invalid input, a repeated save, or a related-record update/delete failure. What constraint or transaction prevents inconsistent records, and how does the user recover?

Evidence: constraint/transaction code, scenario, and expected versus actual results.

D4 · Schema evolution
Design or demonstrate a schema change without silently losing saved history. How do versioning, migration, and test fixtures preserve record relationships?

Evidence: migration code or clearly labeled design, old/new schema, and verification plan/results.

D5 · Filter & aggregate
Show your topic filter and history count/total/average/completion rate. How do edited/deleted records affect derived values and empty-data behavior?

Evidence: query/calculation and a manually checkable dataset.

T · Testing & reliability
T1 · Acceptance coverage
Map tests to both entities' CRUD, the five-screen workflow, settings persistence, and offline operation. Show normal, empty, and invalid cases on two devices/emulators.

Evidence: test matrix, device details, and pass/fail results.

T2 · Failure & recovery
Show a real bug or deliberately tested save/lifecycle failure. What causes it, what feedback appears, and how does the user recover without duplicate or lost records?

Evidence: reproduction steps, fix or tested guard, and regression result.

T3 · Automated tests
Demonstrate unit, widget, and integration tests and explain their different boundaries. Graduate students show at least 10 meaningful unit tests plus widget/integration evidence and the coverage report.

Evidence: test files, commands, results, and an uncovered risk.

T4 · Performance evidence
Measure a list, filter, or aggregate interaction using the same local dataset before and after an optimization (or establish a baseline and justified plan). What changed, and what correctness check prevents a misleading improvement?

Evidence: dataset size, profiling method, measurements, and regression check.

T5 · Deterministic state transitions
How do you test start/progress/finish/reset and lifecycle cleanup without relying on arbitrary waiting? Show controlled inputs or time and prove reset does not leave stale callbacks active.

Evidence: transition rules, fake clock or controlled fixture, and test results.

R · Teamwork, learning & responsibility
R1 · Authentic ownership
Explain your contribution with a feature branch, meaningful commits, and a review. How did the team distribute work and ensure everyone could explain their implementation?

Evidence: task owners, PR/review links, contribution map, and meeting notes.

R2 · Kanban improvement
Show WIP limits, a blocked/aging task, and a completed checkpoint. Which flow observation changed a team policy, and how did the change affect finishing work?

Evidence: dated board snapshots, flow metrics, blocker action, and reflection.

R3 · Learning & documentation
Which course concept changed how you solved a problem? Show where README/architecture/user-guide notes help another developer reproduce or maintain your solution.

Evidence: concept-to-code mapping, setup verification, and one documented limitation.

R4 · Required smart helper & critical thinking Required
Demonstrate your local helper's input, suggestion, explanation, override, and no-data/invalid feedback. Why did you choose these inputs and this method? Compare with your baseline, show a counterexample, and justify a revision or retained limitation. Undergraduates show five case types; graduates additionally show two-method results on at least five held-out valid cases. Label rules versus ML accurately.

Evidence: AI_FEATURE.md, expected/actual case table, metric/sample size, local computation and offline/restart, user control, and individual contribution. AI_Usage_Log.md is separate and needed only if an AI development assistant was used.

R5 · Next iteration
Which planning choice created a limitation, and what would you change first? Propose an improvement that preserves local-only storage and equal topic scope.

Evidence: decision log, prioritized task, owner, and verification plan.
